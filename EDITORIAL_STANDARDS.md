# Aldier and GENIVY — Editorial and institutional communications standard

**Scope:** All public pages, publications, partner materials, news items, forms and digital products. **Status:** Working editorial policy; it is not an adopted constitution or a legal opinion. **Review date:** 2 November 2026.

## The governing distinction

GENIVY is an independent student-centred guild *in development*. Its mission is to expand how students participate in the economy and community of their university, not to take ownership of that economy. The campus-as-city argument should be the overarching economic thesis. The Guild is a community of membership and meaningful participation; GENIVY Originals are proposed student-managed enterprises; independent traders remain independent; programmes and associations are distinct; property, work-study, finance and external opportunities each require their own legal and financial structures.

## Editorial discipline

The following principles synthesize widely established newsroom practices (accuracy, attribution, restraint and clear leads) and evidence-led institutional website writing. They are our editorial rules, not endorsements from or affiliations with any named media or consultancy.

1. **Put the substantive point first.** The opening paragraph answers what the proposition is, to whom it matters and why. Avoid long introductions about being visionary, global, world-leading or revolutionary.
2. **Write for the reader's question.** A student needs belonging and real opportunities; a university needs risk, benefits and approval boundaries; a trader needs a clear service and the preservation of independence; a philanthropist needs a credible theory of change, costs and oversight; a journalist needs verifiable facts and dates.
3. **Give proof, not adjectives.** Describe a student manager's budget authority, a commercial service's contract, or a university approval procedure instead of claiming to be powerful, transformational or unmatched.
4. **Use accurate status language.** Distinguish `proposed`, `under consultation`, `approved`, `contracted`, `funded`, `launched` and `measured`. Never imply that an instrument is adopted merely because it is drafted or published. No unattributed impact metrics or future milestones stated as achieved.
5. **Separate economic participation from ownership.** GENIVY can manage its own enterprises and lawfully leased facilities. It does not control or own students' unions, independent businesses, public institutions or the campus economy itself.
6. **Make the financial logic legible.** Who pays? Who employs? Who delivers? Who owns the facility? What is the liability? Can the programme operate without subsidy? A benefit that lacks a payer is a proposal, not a promise.
7. **Keep the words simple but not the ideas small.** Prefer `student-managed enterprises` over `end-to-end ecosystem activation`; `commercial premises` over `revolutionary infrastructure`. Retain `Guild`, `GENIVY Originals`, `Enterprise Relations`, `Opportunity Partners` and `campus economy` as properly defined terms.
8. **Do not manufacture authority.** Do not describe hypothetical partners, unappointed directors, draft policies, investment forecasts, pending registration or illustrative photographs as proof of operations or institutional standing.
9. **Preserve distinctive design where it helps comprehension.** The opening film, day-in-the-life sequence, economic circulation visual, enterprise portfolio and programme storytelling can remain; each must have accessible content, non-misleading explanatory text and a static/reduced-motion fallback.
10. **Avoid legal and technical ambiguity.** Qualified professionals, accredited bodies, employers and universities keep statutory powers. GENIVY's proposed practitioner councils do not issue legally recognised qualifications simply by choosing a standard.
11. **Attribute external comparisons.** Precedents such as Harvard Student Agencies, Cornell Student Agencies, Unitemps, AIESEC, BRAC, Rotary and large holding foundations are informative but are not GENIVY partners unless formally agreed. Be explicit about comparable and non-comparable aspects.
12. **Edit with operational humility.** Public-facing narratives may be ambitious; contracts, rates, margins, tenure rights and programme promises require validated schedules and formal approvals.

## Recommended writing pattern by page

- **Homepage:** one memorable thesis, a short plain-English proposition, credible routes into participation, then links to detail; no invented operating statistics or repetitive sections.
- **What We Do:** campus-as-city thesis, who participates, how work and enterprise translate into outcomes, the role of partners and community, then evidence and further reading.
- **Enterprises:** explain Originals and the actual responsibilities of student managers, then clearly labelled proposed service portfolio and viability conditions.
- **Enterprise Relations:** distinct commercial function managing existing independent enterprises, clients, suppliers and service contracts. No unpriced promise to 'support every business'.
- **Opportunity Partners:** external providers of internships, training, fellowships or jobs under their own rules. No implied sponsorship or placement without an agreement.
- **Programmes and community:** protect the Guild's purpose beyond wages. Explain who leads, what is delivered, its funding and association independence.
- **Spaces:** separate Centre, Rows, Pavilions, possible housing, tenant relations and property rights; do not suggest that sites or rents are secured.
- **About and governance:** describe draft vs adopted bodies, the legal hierarchy, student management autonomy, trustee independence and Aldier's carefully bounded founding role.
- **Impact and reports:** methodology and verified outcomes first; simulations identified as illustrative with dated assumptions.
- **Contact and partnerships:** a realistic next step. Promises to respond in a fixed period require staffed systems and service standards.

## Source-of-truth hierarchy

Law and binding agreements → formally adopted governing instruments → approved budgets and operating manuals → current documented operating status → consultation proposals → historical PDFs and illustrative financial scenarios. Public wording follows the highest verified source available. Conflicting documents must be reconciled; never silently treat a website paragraph as legal adoption.

## Audit scope and edit decisions

- **GENIVY:** editorial inventory of 97 source-content pages, including institutional hubs, approximately two dozen enterprise profiles, 17 sector profiles, programme pages, partnerships and publications. Rewrite high-consequence hubs and claims; preserve useful specifics in individual service and sector pages while clearly identifying every proposed service. The library's older financial PDFs remain historical, not adopted current accounts.
- **Aldier:** 49 HTML source pages, including five practices, industry pages, GENIVY case study, social impact, newsroom, professional standards and publications. Keep the direct question-and-answer layout of industry/practice pages. Correct overclaimed institutional results and unsupported guarantees. Preserve strong practical copy about decision rights and organisational change.
- **Neither site:** editorial verification alone proves legal incorporation, an approved banking facility, active employment services, property control, audited impact, or whether all Cloudflare browser interactions work.

## Publishing checklist

Before publishing a consequential claim: identify source, date, owner, legal/financial status, responsible reviewer and user-facing evidence. Confirm that a launch date, partnership, title, location, amount or outcome is still true. For major changes, test rendered pages on real devices and verify site forms, accessibility, links and performance. Require a separate legal or finance reviewer where appropriate.

## Public narrative to preserve

**Your campus. Your economy.** This speaks to the student who belongs and participates; it is not a legal statement of collective ownership. GENIVY's institutional ambition is to build the Guild, enterprises, opportunities, support services and physical capacity that enable students to participate in campus economic and civic life across generations.
